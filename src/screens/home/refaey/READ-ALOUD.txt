5. EXPLAINERS REFAEY CAN OPEN (existing text)
---------------------------------------------------
low-sun: Why is the Sun so low? The Moon's axis is tilted only about 1.5°, so near the south pole the Sun never climbs much higher than that. It circles the horizon instead of rising overhead, and a nearby ridge can hide it completely.

farside: Why can’t the far side see Earth? The Moon keeps nearly the same face toward Earth, so about 41% of its surface never sees Earth at all. Landers on most of the far side need a relay satellite to call home, like China's Queqiao for the Chang'e 4 lander.

upright-panels: Why do polar solar panels stand upright? With the Sun skimming the horizon, its light hits a flat panel almost edge-on and delivers only a few percent of its power. Standing the panel upright faces the Sun head-on, so polar landers mount panels vertically and turn them as the Sun circles the horizon.
